Water Damage We See Most in Murfreesboro

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Murfreesboro households. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Murfreesboro, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Murfreesboro homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
